Our story

A bench, a lathe,
and a belief.

METRA began in 2009 in a single room above a bookbinder's shop in Geneva. Muhammad Sohail Metra — a watchmaker trained in Le Locle — had one bench, one lathe and a conviction that had fallen out of fashion: a watch should be finished by the person who signs it.

Seventeen years later, that conviction has not changed. We still assemble no more than eight movements a day. Bridges are bevelled by hand under a loupe. Every dial is inspected three times before it is cased. And every watch that leaves the atelier carries the name of the person who built it.

The chronology

Seventeen years

No shortcuts, no acquisitions — just a small atelier that grew one watch at a time.

2009

A bench in Geneva

Muhammad Sohail Metra opens a one-room atelier above a bookbinder's shop on the left bank of the Rhône.

2014

The first calibre

After five years of prototyping, the METRA 01 automatic movement is completed — 42 hours of reserve, finished entirely by hand.

2018

The Sovereign

Our signature chronograph launches in a limited run of 100 pieces. It sells out in eleven days, without a single advertisement.

2021

Rue du Rhône

The first METRA boutique opens at 12 Rue du Rhône, with a glass workshop where clients can watch their watch being assembled.

2024

One thousand collectors

The maison passes 1,000 owners worldwide and introduces lifetime servicing for every piece ever made.

2026

The Obsidian Skeleton

Our most complex piece to date — 250 numbered examples, each with a hand-openworked movement and a signed case back.

What we stand for

Three principles

Patience

A movement takes four weeks to assemble and regulate. We have never once tried to make that faster.

Provenance

Every component is traceable to its maker. Every case back is numbered and registered to its first owner.

Permanence

We service every METRA ever made, for as long as it exists. A watch is not a product; it is an heirloom.
